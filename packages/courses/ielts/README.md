# IELTS Prep track: research notes

Roadmap-only stage: every node is `contentStatus: "coming_soon"`. The main path is **IELTS Academic**; General Training (GT) differences are noted below and covered by one optional node (`ielts-gt-letter`).

## Current format (checked October 2026)

Listening and Speaking are the same for Academic and GT. Reading and Writing differ.

| Section | Time | Structure | Official question/task types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | ~30 min | 4 parts, 10 questions each = 40. Parts 1–2 everyday social situations, Parts 3–4 educational/training contexts. Heard **once** only. | Multiple choice; Matching; Plan/map/diagram labelling; Form/note/table/flow-chart completion; Sentence completion; Short-answer questions |
| Reading (Academic) | 60 min | 3 passages, 2,150–2,750 words in total, 40 questions | Multiple choice; Identifying information (True/False/Not Given); Identifying writer's views/claims (Yes/No/Not Given); Matching information; Matching headings; Matching features; Matching sentence endings; Sentence completion; Summary/note/table/flow-chart completion; Diagram label completion; Short-answer questions |
| Writing (Academic) | 60 min | Task 1 (≥150 words, ~20 min): describe a graph/chart/table or a diagram of a process, object or event (maps are common). Task 2 (≥250 words, ~40 min): essay responding to a point of view, argument or problem. Task 2 counts more than Task 1. | Criteria: Task achievement (T1) / Task response (T2); Coherence and cohesion; Lexical resource; Grammatical range and accuracy |
| Speaking | 11–14 min | Face-to-face with an examiner, recorded. Part 1 Introduction and interview (4–5 min); Part 2 Long turn: task card, 1 min preparation, up to 2 min talk (3–4 min total); Part 3 Discussion (4–5 min). | Criteria: Fluency and coherence; Lexical resource; Grammatical range and accuracy; Pronunciation |

Total: about 2 h 45 min. Listening, Reading and Writing are taken in one sitting; Speaking may be on the same day or up to 7 days before/after.

### Where General Training differs
- **Reading:** same 60 min, 40 questions and the same question types, but texts are everyday and workplace texts in 3 sections of rising difficulty: (1) "social survival" texts such as notices, adverts and timetables; (2) workplace texts such as job descriptions, contracts and training material; (3) one longer general text (magazine/newspaper article, book extract). GT raw-to-band conversion is stricter (for example ~30/40 ≈ band 6 in GT vs band 7 in Academic).
- **Writing Task 1:** a **letter** (≥150 words) responding to a situation, covering three bullet points, in a personal, semi-formal or formal tone; must be continuous prose. Task 2 is an essay as in Academic (topics are more general).
- **Use:** GT is for migration and work/training; Academic for university admission and professional registration. IELTS Online (at home) is Academic only.

### Delivery: the 2026 change
- On 5 March 2026 IELTS announced that **paper-based IELTS ends from mid-2026**; all tests move to computer, with dates varying by market. The skills tested, test construct and interpretation of results are unchanged. Speaking stays face-to-face with an examiner.
- An optional **"Writing on Paper"** mode (computer test, handwritten Writing) is being offered in selected markets from mid-2026; not for IELTS for UKVI. Indonesia was **not** listed on the page I checked.
- On computer, Listening answers are typed during the test, so the extra 10-minute transfer time of the paper test no longer applies (IDP Indonesia).
- **Indonesia:** British Council Indonesia said it would stop paper IELTS from **21 June 2026**; IDP Indonesia says all IELTS tests move to computer **from July 2026** (IELTS Life Skills stays on paper). The two providers give different dates, but either way paper IELTS has ended in Indonesia by October 2026. The track therefore teaches the computer-delivered test only.
- **IELTS Online** (at home, Speaking by video call) exists in selected countries, Academic only. I could not confirm whether it is currently bookable in Indonesia (IDP Indonesia links to a booking page but gives no details).

## Scoring and CEFR
- Each section gets a band from 0 to 9 in whole or half bands. Overall band = average of the four, rounded to the nearest half band; an average ending in .25 rounds up to the next half band, and .75 rounds up to the next whole band (e.g. 6.25 → 6.5, 6.75 → 7.0, 6.125 → 6.0).
- Listening and Reading are marked out of 40 and converted. Published approximate averages: Listening band 6 ≈ 23/40, band 7 ≈ 30/40; Academic Reading band 6 ≈ 23/40, band 7 ≈ 30/40; GT Reading band 6 ≈ 30/40. Exact thresholds vary slightly between test versions.
- **CEFR mapping published by IELTS (ielts.org):** IELTS 5.5–6.5 ≈ **B2**; 7–8 ≈ **C1**; band 9 ≈ **C2** (approximate). IELTS says one-to-one comparisons aren't possible and that concordance tables should be treated with caution. Lower bands (B1 ≈ 4–5) appear only on IDP regional pages, not in the ielts.org text I could read, so the B1 boundary isn't treated as official here. Cambridge's own linking study puts C1 nearer 6.5–7.0, so the C1 boundary is approximate too.

## Who takes it in Indonesia
Run in Indonesia by IDP and British Council (British Council Foundation Indonesia). The main use is **Academic** for study abroad (Australia, UK, Netherlands, etc.) and scholarship applications. **GT** is used for migration and work (for example to Australia or Canada). Typical targets are 6.0–7.0 overall, sometimes with a minimum per section. This is general knowledge; I did not check individual scholarship requirements (for example LPDP) against an official source.

## How the track's levels map to the test
`recommendedAfter`: English course **B1**. Target bands 6.0–7.0 sit around B2–C1, so a learner should be at a solid B1 before starting.

| Level id | Covers | Nodes |
|---|---|---|
| `Start` | Format, Academic vs GT, computer delivery, scoring; paraphrasing; academic vocabulary | 3 |
| `Listening` | Strategy + one node per official question type (sentence completion and short-answer merged) | 6 |
| `Reading` | Skimming/scanning + official types, grouped: TFNG/YNNG; Matching headings; Matching information/features/sentence endings; all completion types; MCQ + short answer | 6 |
| `Writing` | Academic Task 1 (data; process/map), Task 2 (prompt analysis; essay), criteria; **GT letter (optional, not required for Mock)** | 6 |
| `Speaking` | Parts 1, 2, 3 + fluency/pronunciation (criteria) | 4 |
| `Mock` | Timed section practice, full mock, score review & test-day plan | 3 |

Practice scores in the app are **estimates**, never official bands (the tutor policy says so explicitly).
